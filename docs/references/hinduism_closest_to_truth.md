## I. Introduction: The Problem of Religious Equivalence {#introduction}

In a remarkable interview, scholar of religion **Jeffrey Kripal** articulates what he calls "**the problem of believing too much**":

> "The Christian says to the Buddhist, 'You're either a demon or you are a lesser revelation.' The Buddhist says the same thing about the Christian... I'm completely convinced that people levitated. I'm completely convinced people have near-death experiences. And 2 years ago, I witnessed a Christian miracle, but I still did not convert... because of the existence of other genuine miracles in competing religious traditions."

This is the **crisis of religious equivalence** (isostheneia, ἰσοσθένεια):

**The Problem:**
- Multiple religious traditions make **mutually exclusive truth claims**
- Each tradition has **genuine miraculous phenomena**
- Each has **authentic mystical experiences**
- Each explains the others as "demons" or "lesser revelations"

**Kripal's Response:**
- Reject **all exclusivist claims**
- Adopt a **comparative, non-committal stance**
- "I've spent my life looking at all these traditions. Good luck. You're not going to find a tradition that has all your answers."

---

**Hindu Solution:** **Graded Inclusivism (Adhikāra-bheda)**

Hinduism has a **hierarchical but non-exclusive** model:

```
LEVELS OF UNDERSTANDING (from conventional to ultimate):

Level 1: KARMA-KĀṆḌA (Ritual Action)
   - Worship of devatās for worldly benefits
   - Rituals, sacrifices, prayers
   - Valid for those at this stage (adhikāra)

Level 2: UPĀSANA-KĀṆḌA (Devotional Meditation)
   - Devotion to Īśvara (Personal God)
   - Bhakti yoga
   - Christianity, Islam, Vaiṣṇavism fit here

Level 3: JÑĀNA-KĀṆḌA (Knowledge of Non-Duality)
   - Advaita Vedānta: Brahman = Ātman
   - Direct realization: "Aham Brahmāsmi" (I am Brahman)
   - Buddhism's emptiness partially overlaps here

Level 4: TURĪYA (The Fourth State)
   - Beyond even non-duality
   - Pure consciousness without subject-object duality
```

**Critical Difference:**

- **Abrahamic/Buddhist:** Level 3 NEGATES Levels 1-2 ("You were WRONG before")
- **Hindu:** Level 3 INCLUDES Levels 1-2 ("You were RIGHT for your stage")

**Example:**

A Christian worshipping Jesus as savior is NOT "wrong" or "demonic" in Hinduism — they are at **Level 2**, which is **perfectly valid** for their current spiritual development (adhikāra).

A Buddhist realizing emptiness is NOT "wrong" — they've reached **Level 3**, but may not yet have integrated the personal (Īśvara) dimension.

1. **Same underlying Reality** (Brahman) responds to sincere practice

Brahman = Nirguṇa (without attributes) + Saguṇa (with attributes)

**Both dimensions are REAL.** Both are aspects of Brahman.

**This is the ONLY framework that explains all the data without ad hoc dismissals.**

2. To limited witnesses

4. With no recording technology

**You MUST take them on faith.**

**Kripal's Critique:**

> "Why are you giving so much authority to a bunch of texts written thousands of years ago? Why not root it in human experience and in the human being?"

**This is devastating to Abrahamic claims.**

**Instead:**

**"The ṛṣis (seers) DISCOVERED eternal truths through direct perception (aparokṣa-jñāna)."**

**Anyone can replicate their experiments.**

### C. Hinduism's Resolution: Both/And (Ubhayavāda)

**Advaita Vedānta's Sophisticated Position:**

**Two Levels of Truth (Satyadvaya):**

1. **Vyāvahārika Satya** (Conventional/Empirical Truth)
   - The self/ego DOES exist (empirically)
   - Reincarnation DOES occur
   - Personal gods DO respond to prayers

2. **Pāramārthika Satya** (Ultimate Truth)
   - The self is ultimately Brahman (Ātman = Brahman)
   - No separate individual exists
   - Only One without a second (Ekam eva advitīyam)

**BOTH are true — at their respective levels.**

**Buddhism's Error (from Vedāntic perspective):**

Buddhism **conflates** the two levels:

- Correctly sees: No **permanent, independent** self exists
- Incorrectly concludes: **No self at all** exists

**Vedānta's Correction:**

- **Empirical self** (jīvātman) exists **conventionally**
- **Ultimate Self** (Paramātman/Brahman) exists **absolutely**
- **Individual self** (ahaṅkāra) is **neither real nor unreal** (mithyā)

**This explains ALL of Kripal's data:**

✅ Reincarnation memories (empirical continuity of jīvātman)
✅ Near-death experiences (transitional states of consciousness)
✅ Personality persists after death (subtle body continues)
✅ But ultimately dissolves (mokṣa = liberation from individual existence)

**Buddhist position only explains HALF the data** (the ultimate dissolution), not the empirical continuity.
